Companies are deploying agents with standing permissions, no ledger, and no way to take an authorization back. That is how a helpful assistant becomes an unaccountable actor. AgenticBiz is the services firm that deploys agents properly: every agent with a binding, every action with a record, every grant revocable.

The problem we walk into

In most companies, the gap is the same: what agents are supposed to do and what they can actually do are two different lists. Permissions accumulate. Nobody owns the list. The first time anyone reads it is after something goes wrong. We find the gap before it finds you.

Standing permissions

Agents granted access once, never reviewed, still holding it months later. Authority that was never meant to be permanent, quietly becoming permanent.

No ledger

Actions taken with no durable record — no answer to the simplest question after an incident: who authorized that, and when?

No way back

Grants that can’t be revoked cleanly, or at all. An authorization you can’t take back isn’t an authorization — it’s a surrender.

What we do

Deploy

Design and deploy agents in real workflows — support, operations, research, back office — scoped to the job, not to the tool’s maximum reach. An agent gets the authority its task needs, and nothing it doesn’t.

Govern

Install the envelope around every deployment: permissions named exactly, budgets and expiries on every grant, attenuation at every hop, a ledger that survives the agent. Governance is part of the deployment, not a review after it.

Audit

Review the agents you already have: what they can actually do versus what they’re supposed to do, formal authority versus effective authority, in a report you can act on. Most companies are surprised by the first audit. That’s the point.
